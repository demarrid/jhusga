# Recovering JHU student-government records, 2000–2020

Research date: November 13, 2026.

**Best starting points:** the recovered 2013 minutes and judiciary statement; Evan Lazerowitz for the old website and 2008 constitution; Bill Smedick for the institutional gap; Stephen Goutman for the 2002 reform; Jered Ede for the 2003 constitution; and the 2012–2015 secretaries for minutes and website migrations.

## Scope and limits

Screened the stored text and metadata of all 1,300 archive entries with `datedAt` from January 1, 2000 through December 31, 2020, using targeted searches for documents, links, governing changes, recordkeeping, websites, and officers, then read the relevant passages and checked external leads. Of these entries, 754 are News-Letter articles and 546 are other records. This is a search of dated entries, not a claim that every undated/misdated record or every historical newspaper article has been examined. No 2000 entries were returned. The repository’s newspaper backfill begins in 2001, so 2000 needs a separate print/archive search.

The dated pre-2015 set contains only four non-newspaper records: 2008 CSE rules, a 2012 catering agreement, February 2013 bylaws, and a 2013 class-event record. The February 2013 bylaws are therefore **already held**, not a new discovery. None of the newly recovered files below appeared among those four. No production records were changed and no outreach was sent.

Status vocabulary: **Recovered** = downloaded PDF and contents checked; **documented lead** = a contemporary source identifies the document, site, or custodian, but the target has not been recovered; **search hypothesis** = a reasonable next search, not evidence of holdings. Historical student addresses are labeled separately from currently published professional addresses.

## 5. Repositories and request strategy

- **University Archives:** the [Student Council finding aid](https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/1072) describes constitution copies only through 1992 in its narrative. Do not assume its description proves a 2000s collection. Ask specifically about later/unprocessed accessions and adjacent administrative collections. The [Homewood Student Affairs web-archive listing](https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/1529/digitized) is another route, but the listed website series begins in 2015 and is not a verified pre-2015 SGA collection.
- **University web preservation:** send the historical hosts and the confirmed 2013 migration clue together. Ask archivists to check Archive-It crawl exclusions, off-domain attachments, retained WARC files, and retired web-server exports. These are proposed retrieval methods, not confirmed available backups.
- **News-Letter:** ask for 2000 print issues and original source files, not merely another copy of online reporting. The library’s general page describes its digitized News-Letter collection as 1897–1990, so it does not establish digital coverage of 2000. Early web articles may have lost attachments and links during migrations.
- **FIRE and case participants:** 2013 is unusually promising because third parties kept primary documents. Ask whether their retained case packet contains the governing documents used at the time; do not infer that it does from quoted clauses.
- **Student organizations and SAC:** recognized organizations may still hold budget notices, authorization letters, Blue Books, and attached governing documents. Ask central office custodians first, then a few long-running organizations, rather than broadcasting to every group.
- **Later SGA handoffs:** ask 2014–2017 recordkeepers for the oldest folder/export in their possession. A 2017 migration said it reached three years back, making it especially relevant to the missing 2014 bridge.

For each outreach request, give the person’s year/role, two or three exact document names, and the contemporary article link. Ask for original Word/PDF files, email attachments, website/database exports, or a referral. Request any related drafts too, while preserving draft/adopted distinctions. For site builders, include the obsolete URLs so they can search old disks or mail.
