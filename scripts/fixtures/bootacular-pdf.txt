SECTION 1. TITLE.
This Act may be referred to as the "Bootacular Baltimore Funding Bill".
SECTION 2. DEFINITIONS.
(a) SGA - The term “SGA” shall refer to the Student Government Association of The
Johns Hopkins University.
(b) JHU, Hopkins - The terms “JHU” and “Hopkins” shall refer to the Johns Hopkins
University.
(c) CARE - The term "CARE" shall refer to the student-run organization under the
Center for Social Concern committed to encouraging community exploration in
safe and fun ways.
(d) CSC - The term “CSC” shall refer to the Center for Social Concern of The Johns
Hopkins University.
SECTION 3. FINDINGS AND PURPOSES.
Findings: Currently, JHU lacks a student-run Trick-or-Treating event that engages the
broader Baltimore community. While faculty have hosted similar activities on campus
for the past few years, these events often go unnoticed and receive low engagement.
There is demonstrated interest in creating opportunities for students to engage with
each other and the local Baltimore community during the fall season.
Purpose: The purpose of this bill is to fund "Bootacular Baltimore," a collaborative
event between SGA's Programming Council, CARE, and CSC designed to facilitate
meaningful student engagement with each other and the Baltimore community through
a structured Trick-or-Treating experience.
Additional Information:
The event is a revamped version of Trick-or-Treating designed to create
meaningful connections between JHU students and Baltimore's broader community.
This student-run partnership between LEED (through the Programming Council) and
the CSC (through CARE, an established RSO) addresses a gap in JHU's fall
programming.
The multi-part event, spanning 2 to 3 hours in the evening, is limited to 150
students organized into 10 groups of 10 to 15 people each. In the first hour, students
arrive to check in, receive stickers from all affiliated organizations (SGA, CARE, CSC,
and LEED), and decorate costume-related items like headbands and masks at outdoor
tables. This creates immediate engagement and fosters a sense of belonging from the
start so that students can form these large groups.
In the second hour, student groups are handed paper cards to track attendance
and small organza bags to collect candy as they embark on the Bootacular Community
Walk, a specially designed trick-or-treating route through ~10 predefined community
organizations that partner with the CSC. CARE volunteers and Programming Council
members, trained by CSC faculty on safety and route mapping, will guide groups
through the route using maps and flashlights. At each stop, a community organization
representative speaks for 2-4 minutes about their mission and role in Baltimore,
followed by a stamp on the paper card and candy from community partners. To
incentivize full participation, students who collect all 10 stamps receive a branded tote
